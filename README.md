## Practical positioning

A strong image prompt is not decoration. It is a mini design brief.

A good prompt answers:

- What is the image for?
- Who is it for?
- What is the subject?
- How should it be framed?
- What should the mood be?
- What details matter?
- What should be avoided?

Weak prompt:

```text
A beautiful luxury product image, realistic, highly detailed, cinematic.
```

Better prompt:

```text
Create a premium studio product image of a matte black smartwatch standing upright on a dark reflective surface.
Composition: centered product hero shot, slightly angled three-quarters toward the camera, balanced negative space around the subject.
Environment: minimalist charcoal studio background with a subtle gradient.
Lighting: soft directional light from the upper left with controlled edge highlights.
Details: sharp watch edges, crisp band texture, clean reflections and modern luxury feel.
Purpose: e-commerce hero image and ad creative.
Mood: premium, modern and high-tech.
Restrictions: no extra objects, no fake text, no watermark, no distorted watch face.
```

## Seedream 5 prompt formula

Use this structure for most image generation tasks:

```text
Create a [style / medium] image of [subject].
Composition: [framing, crop, angle, layout].
Environment: [background or scene].
Lighting: [type, direction, mood].
Details: [textures, colors, focus points].
Purpose: [ad creative, product page, social post, poster, concept art, source frame].
Mood: [emotion or brand feel].
Restrictions: [what to avoid].
```

Example:

```text
Create a realistic premium product image of a glass skincare bottle with a silver dropper cap.
Composition: centered on a clean light stone surface, front-facing with slight three-quarter angle, enough negative space around the product.
Environment: soft minimal bathroom scene with blurred neutral background.
Lighting: soft daylight from the left with gentle highlights on the glass.
Details: translucent serum, crisp label area, subtle reflection, elegant texture and high-end beauty aesthetic.
Purpose: e-commerce product hero image.
Mood: clean, luxurious and trustworthy.
Restrictions: no extra bottles, no fake text, no warped reflections, no watermarks.
```

## Commercial image prompt structure

For product, ad and marketing visuals, this simple structure works well:

```text
1. Subject
2. Composition
3. Environment
4. Lighting
5. Detail cues
6. Intended use
7. Restrictions
```

Template:

```text
Create an image of [subject].
Composition: [how it is framed].
Environment: [where it is].
Lighting: [what kind of light].
Details: [materials, textures, key focus].
Use: [what it is for].
Restrictions: [what must not happen].
```

## Editing prompt formula

If you are editing an image, use this:

```text
Edit the image to [specific change].
Preserve [what must stay the same].
Improve [what should become better].
Style: [desired style].
Restrictions: [what to avoid].
```

Example:

```text
Edit the image to replace the cluttered background with a clean soft beige studio backdrop.
Preserve the product shape, color, label position and overall angle.
Improve the lighting so the product appears brighter and more premium.
Style: commercial beauty photography.
Restrictions: no fake text, no extra products, no distortion.
```

## Common mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using empty adjectives

Words like “amazing” or “best quality” are weak unless supported by actual visual instructions.

### Mistake 2: No composition direction

If you do not tell the model how to frame the subject, it may choose an unhelpful crop.

### Mistake 3: Ignoring purpose

A poster, product image and thumbnail need different layouts.

### Mistake 4: Too many competing details

One image should usually have one main subject and one main visual story.

### Mistake 5: Letting AI text do the heavy lifting

If typography matters, add it manually after generation when possible.

### Mistake 6: Not creating variants

The best workflow is usually one strong base prompt plus several controlled variations.

### Mistake 7: Skipping polish

A good image often gets better after cleanup, minor edits and upscale.

## FAQ

### What are Seedream 5 prompts?

Seedream 5 prompts are structured instructions used to create images in a Seedream-related workflow. In practice, the best prompts describe the subject, composition, environment, lighting, details, purpose and restrictions.

### How do I write a better Seedream prompt?

Start with what the image is for. Then describe the subject, framing, environment, lighting and important detail cues. Finally, add restrictions for common failure modes.

### What makes a good product image prompt?

A good product prompt includes product type, angle, background, lighting, material detail, intended use and restrictions such as no label distortion or extra objects.

### Can I use Seedream-style prompts for ad creative?

Yes. Many ad visuals begin as image prompts. You can generate hero images, social creatives, campaign visuals and source frames.

### Can I edit images as well?

Yes. A good editing prompt says exactly what to change, what to preserve and what to avoid.

### How do I turn a good image into a video?

First create a strong source frame with clear depth and subject focus. Then use it in an image-to-video workflow.

### Is readable text reliable?

Generated text can still be inconsistent. For important copy, add it manually after generation when possible.

### What should I avoid?

Avoid brand imitation, fake testimonials, copyrighted characters, deceptive realism, fake documents and misleading before-and-after claims.

### Can I use these prompts commercially?

Commercial use depends on the platform terms, model terms, your input rights, output rights, copyright, trademark and advertising rules. Always review before publishing.
